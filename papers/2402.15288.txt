Abstract—In this work, we present a high-throughput field
programmable gate array (FPGA) demonstrator of an artificial
neural network (ANN)-based equalizer. The equalization is per-
formed and illustrated in real-time for a 30 GBd, two-level pulse
amplitude modulation (PAM2) optical communication system.
Index Terms—FPGA, ANN, Optical Communications
I. I NTRODUCTION
Short-reach optical communication systems must be cost
and power efficient while satisfying the increasing demand of
data rates. For these reasons, they typically rely on intensity-
modulation and direct-detection (IM/DD) of the optical signal.
In such systems, the achievable data rate is limited due to
severe nonlinear signal impairments caused for example by
low-cost components such as electro-absorption modulators
(EAMs) and semiconductor optical amplifiers (SOAs), or by
nonlinear channel effects caused, e.g., by chromatic dispersion
(CD) with square-law detection.
Digital linear equalization can compensate for such non-
linear distortions only to a certain extent. Thus, nonlinear
equalization methods draw the attention of the community. In
particular, machine learning using artificial neural networks
(ANNs), which proved to be highly capable of compensating
for nonlinear impairments, is a contender for future equaliz-
ers [1]. However, compared to conventional algorithms, which
have been optimized for decades, ANNs often introduce high
computational complexity, limiting the achievable throughput
of the hardware implementation. Previous works showed that
even with advanced field programmable gate array (FPGA)
platforms, it is challenging to meet the strict performance
requirements of optical communication systems [2], [3].
In this work, we present an FPGA demonstrator that tackles
the challenges of real-time ANN-based equalization by fea-
turing an optimized ANN topology and a highly parallelized
hardware architecture. The optical two-level pulse amplitude
modulation (PAM2) signal is transmitted at 30 GBdover a

II. S CIENTIFIC AND TECHNICAL DESCRIPTION
In the following, we provide a short overview of our work,
starting from the algorithm and its implementation down to
the actual demonstrator. For the interested reader, we refer to
the provided references for a more detailed explanation.
A. ANN Topology
In contrast to classical linear equalizers based on finite
impulse response (FIR) filters, the demonstrator features an
ANN-based equalizer. Specifically, we choose a convolutional
neural network (CNN) as neural network type since it resem-
bles the structure of conventional digital filters. In particular,
the model consists of 3 convolutional layers with a kernel
size of 9 where the first two layers are followed by batch-
normalization and ReLU activation function. The feature maps
of the first two layers consist of 5 channels while the last
feature map has 8 channels. Correspondingly, the first layer
has a stride of 8, which allows for the processing of 8 symbols
with one run of the network.
The model has been selected based on an extensive design
space exploration, described in detail in [4]. As a result, the
ANN achieves around one order of magnitude lower BER than
a linear digital filter with the same computational complexity.
B. FPGA ANN Implementation
For FPGA implementation of the ANN, Vivado HLS in
combination with Vivado 2022.2 is used. The hardware ar-
chitecture is based on arbitrary precision fixed-point format
for weights and activations while the 6-bit inputs and outputs
are transferred using the AXI-Stream interface.
The main target of our demonstrator is to satisfy the high-
throughput constraints of the 30 GBdoptical communication
channel. Thus, the aim of our hardware architecture is to utilize
the available resources efficiently by boosting parallelism on
all implementation levels. The first level of parallelism is based
on our streaming hardware architecture, where each of the

C. Demonstrator
The experimental setup including the digital signal pro-
cessing of the receiver is depicted in Fig. 1. We use the
Keysight USPA prototyping platform, which contains a Xilinx
VU9P FPGA on transmitter and receiver side, and a 60 GSa/s
digital-to-analog converter (DAC) and analog-to-digital con-
verter (ADC) with 6 bitresolution. At the transmitter side, a
30 GBd, non-return-to-zero (NRZ), PAM2 signal is generated
and drives the EAM to modulate the optical carrier at1540 nm,
which is provided by an distributed-feedback (DFB) laser. The
optical signal is then transmitted over a 20.56 kmSSMF to
accumulate CD. At the receiver side, the signal is detected
by a photodiode and amplified by a transimpedance amplifier.
Afterwards, the received signal is sampled by an ADC and
processed by the receiver FPGA. As part of the digital signal
processing of the receiver, the twofold oversampled received
signal is first synchronized to the transmit clock utilizing a
feedforward clock recovery architecture as described in [5].
Afterwards, the signal is equalized using our high-throughput
ANN-based equalizer. Finally, we implement the decisioning
and BER tester to evaluate the signal quality and BER. This
way, the communication performance is visualized in real-time
and the equalizer’s compensation capabilities are showcased.
Figure 2 shows the real-time demonstrator including a
monitor which illustrates the functionality of the ANN-based
equalizer. Before the equalizer, the two levels of the PAM2-
symbols are partly overlapping around the decision threshold
due to inter-symbol interference (ISI), which is caused by CD,
and nonlinear distortions. After the equalizer, the symbols are
well separated leading to a BER close to zero.

III. R ESEARCH CONTRIBUTIONS & C ONCLUSION
The main purpose of our demonstrator is to illustrate how
ANN-based equalizers can be applied to high-throughput opti-
cal communication systems. Thus, the demonstrator forms the
basis for the visualization of multiple contributions of previous
and future works in the field of ANN-based equalization. It
can be utilized to visualize the contributions of [4], where
a similar high-throughput equalizer was implemented on the
Xilinx VU13P FPGA. Another use-case is the visualization of
the novel unsupervised equalization approaches of [6] and [7].
Therefore, in future work, the demonstrator could further be
extended to illustrate the training process of the ANN.
To summarize, the demonstrator is a first step towards
a practical optical communication system featuring ANN-
based equalizers. It shows that, if efficiently implemented,
ANN-based algorithms can satisfy the requirements of high-
throughput communication systems while successfully com-
pensating for nonlinear distortions.
